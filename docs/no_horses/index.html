A Rule Sign, in Cree

No Horses is a trail-management rule turned into a trail name, and the Cree keeps that deadpan sign-board tone. êkâ is the prohibitive 'don't / none' — the 'no' of a posted rule — set in front of misatimwak 'horses.' A softer, plainer version, namôya misatimwak, simply states 'no horses'; the plaque takes the firmer êkâ.

There's a small etymology worth pointing at: misatim 'horse' is literally a 'big dog' — mis- 'big' + atim 'dog.' When horses reached the plains they were named as the larger cousin of the dog that already pulled the travois, so the longer form mistatim and everyday misatim both carry that 'big dog' picture. The same mis- 'big' root shows up across these signs — in mistâpiskihkomân 'machete (big iron knife)' and in 'Too Much' (mistahi).

No Horses runs in the Keillor river-valley network on ᓀᐦᐃᔭᐤ ᐊᐢᑭᕀ Nêhiyaw-Askiy — Plains Cree territory, Treaty 6, and a homeland of the Métis Nation.

Building Other Forms

English: "no horses"
namôya misatimwak
ᓇᒨᔭ ᒥᓴᑎᒧᐠ

namôya 'no / not' + misatimwak 'horses.' The plain statement rather than the prohibitive command.

English: "horses don't go here"
namôya otêhtêwak misatimwak
ᓇᒨᔭ ᐅᑌᐦᑌᐗᐠ ᒥᓴᑎᒧᐠ

Adds a verb of going/being here — a fuller 'horses do not travel this trail.'
