About nîpâmâyâtân pekiseyâhk — the Haze We Ride Through

Purple Haze is a Hendrix song before it is a trail, and the trail name is itself a borrow — when the local builders carved the Cree, they did not try to translate the colour ‘purple’ literally (which Plains Cree does not have a single root for) but instead rendered the FEELING of the song and the trail: the dim, hazy way of moving through. The plaque on the wall is unusually pictorial for the set: a two-tone purple mountain silhouette under a pink-fading-to-violet sky, the syllabics ᓂᐹᒫᔮᑖᐣ ᐯᑭᓭᔮᕽ in clean white above the mountain, ‘PURPLE HAZE’ in white block letters across the violet base.

The Cree breaks into two parts. nîpâmâyâtân is a VII (it-verb): nîpâ‑ ‘in the dark, at night, dimly’ + ‑mâyâtan ‘it is bad / ugly / unclear,’ together meaning roughly ‘it is hazy / dim / hard to see’ (the sense Hendrix gets at with ‘purple haze all in my eyes’). pekiseyâhk is an AI conjunct in the first-person-plural inclusive: ‘when we (all of us) pass through it.’ Read together: ‘when it is hazy and we (all) pass through it’ — the rider’s phrase, not a colour name.

The trail itself is in the Hawrelak/Central Edmonton (North) network, neighbour to Wonder Woman, Lovers Lane, and Victoria’s Secret in the river-valley clusters under ᓀᐦᐃᔭᐤ ᐊᐢᑭᕀ Nêhiyaw‑Askiy (Plains Cree territory). On the wall there are two Purple Haze plaques — a small portrait-format one in row 1 (the one wired here) and a fuller one further right — both reading the same Cree.

Building Other Forms

English: "Night, at night (the bare temporal form)"
tipiskâw
ᑎᐱᐢᑳᐤ

Free-standing verb for ‘it is night.’ The nîpâ‑ initial in the headword is the bound version of this dim/dark concept.

English: "It is bad / it is ugly (VII)"
mâyâtan
ᒫᔮᑖᐣ

‘It is bad/unclear.’ With the night-initial nîpâ‑ it produces ‘it is hazy / it is bad-as-at-night’ — the visibility-blurring sense the trail name plays on.

English: "S/he passes through, rides through"
pekise‑ ‑ew → pekisew / pekisin
ᐯᑭᓭ ᐤ

The independent (3sg) form. The plaque uses the conjunct 1pl-incl pekiseyâhk ‘when we (all) ride through.’
