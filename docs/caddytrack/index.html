The Carrying Trail & Caddyshack

Caddytrack is Forest Heights wordplay on golf and the 1980 film Caddyshack: English folds together caddy (the club carrier), caddie/caddy (the rolling cart), and track (trail and cart-rut). The plaque keeps the person sense in nêhiyawêwin: pêyahikan meskanaw 'carrying/hauling trail' — pêyahikan 'carrying, hauling' + meskanaw 'trail' — so the name is a path for hauling, like a caddy lugging bags, not a wagon road.

Two cart-first renderings stay off the plaque but explain the other jokes: hyphenated otâpânâsko-meskanaw 'vehicle/cart trail' (otâpânâsk 'wagon/cart' + meskanaw) puns on track as something a cart rolls on; plain otâpânâsk meskanaw is the cart's road. That cart reading is what Upper Caddytrack picks up as ispimihk otâpânâsko-meskanaw 'upper cart trail.' The English name also carries a servant/porter overtone — a caddy serves the player — and the screenprint makes the movie wink visible: red lips biting Carl Spackler's bucket-hat face as the golf ball, a trestle bridge behind.

On the same hillside the nicknames answer each other: The Holy Trail (kâ-kihcitwâwik meskanaw) and Shooter McGavin (pâskisikêw Makâpin) share the golf-movie register with Caddytrack; Happy Ending (sîkinikêwin iskwayâk), the Star Wars cluster (Lower Ewok, Master Yoda), and names like Andre's Trail, Rattlesnake, Prime Rib, McAloon R.I.P., and Drop In braid English pop culture into one local web.

Like every sign in this series, Caddytrack sits on ᓀᐦᐃᔭᐤ ᐊᐢᑭᕀ Nêhiyaw-Askiy — Plains Cree territory, Treaty 6, and a homeland of the Métis Nation. These plaques are one way of putting nêhiyawêwin back on the land it was always spoken on.

The Trail

A ~691 m Forest Heights line with about 42 m of net descent and ~36 m of climbing — a short connector in the local web beside The Holy Trail, Upper Caddytrack, and the other nickname trails on this hillside.
